What Amazon account management includes

Full service Amazon account management, the way we run it, is four jobs that change each other. An Amazon account management agency that only sends a monthly PDF is describing the console you already have.

Listings

Titles, bullets, backend search terms, images, and A+ content. A click that lands on a weak page wastes the ad budget and the organic rank behind it. The working detail is on Amazon listing optimization services.

Advertising

Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display, reviewed every week: search terms, negatives, bids, and budgets. That pass lives on the Amazon PPC section. Account management is why the person changing the bid can also change the listing the click lands on.

Inventory and FBA

Weeks of cover, inbound shipments, stranded units, and the storage fees that show up when inventory sits too long or runs out. The full version is Amazon FBA management services.

Account health

Policy warnings, suppressed listings, and performance notifications, read when they arrive. Seller management services fall apart when health is a slide in the monthly deck instead of a daily check.

We do not take on general social media or a website rebuild. We also do not promise a specific ACOS. Across accounts we manage, average ACOS reduction in the first 90 days is 45%. That is an average, not a forecast for the next brand.

Daily, weekly and monthly work

The cadence is the product. An Amazon seller management company that "checks in monthly" is reading the damage after it has posted. This is the rhythm on accounts we run.

What happens on a managed Amazon account, by cadence
CadenceWhat gets done
Daily Account Health and performance notifications. Listings that went inactive or suppressed. Any hero ASIN that will stock out inside its lead time. Problems that cannot wait for the weekly pass.
Weekly Search terms, negatives, bids, and budget moves. Listing fixes where clicks are healthy and conversion is not. Inbound shipment status and the stranded queue. A short note on what changed, including weeks that were quiet and why.
Monthly Sales, TACoS, and campaign ACOS, read together. Catalog priorities for the next listing passes. A restock plan that matches production lead time. The Account Health trend, not a single day's score.

What you should be able to point at: a user login that is yours, a change history that matches the weekly note, and a restock number you approved before a purchase order went to the factory. If those three are missing, the retainer is a reporting fee.

Inventory and FBA

A stockout erases the rank the ads just bought. Extra inventory creates storage fees and, once it ages, aged-inventory fees. Account management includes the replenishment conversation: recent sales rate, what the ad plan will do to that rate, supplier lead time, freight and check-in time, units already at FBA, and units already on a shipment.

We are not a prep warehouse. We plan the shipment in Seller Central, reconcile what Amazon receives, and clear stranded inventory so units you already paid to send can be sold. Physical labeling and polybagging stay with your warehouse or a prep partner you choose. Q4 timing, restock limits, and reimbursement cases are written up on the FBA page, with the deadlines in the Q4 prep checklist.

Reporting

You already have Seller Central and the ads console. A useful monthly note is the decision list: what sold, what the ads cost against total sales, which listings moved, where inventory will break, and what we are doing next. You should be able to approve a budget or a purchase order from that note.

FAQ

What does an Amazon account manager do?

An account manager runs the channel during the week: account health and suppressed listings, the advertising pass, listing fixes that block conversion, and inventory cover so you are not buying rank you cannot supply. You should be able to see those edits in Seller Central and Amazon Ads change history.

How much do account management services cost?

SellTru uses a flat monthly retainer, not a percent of ad spend. Catalog size, whether you need the full account or a slice of it, and whether Walmart is included all change the fee. A single figure on this page would be a guess, so the ranges stay on the agency pricing guide. The audit is free. Ad spend is paid to Amazon and is separate from the retainer.

Do I keep ownership of my account?

Yes. The Seller Central account stays in your name. We are added as a user you can remove. You keep the primary login, the bank account, and the legal entity. If the engagement ends, the catalog, the campaigns, and the FBA inventory stay on your account.

How is this different from PPC management?

PPC management is the weekly work on Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display: search terms, negatives, bids, and budgets. Account management includes that pass and also the listings, FBA inventory, and account health. If ads are the only problem, we will say so and scope the smaller job.

How fast do you start?

The free audit is the start. Once you add us as a user, the first week covers account health, inventory cover, and the worst wasted search terms. There is a 90-day onboarding period because marketplace results need time. After that the agreement is month to month.

Do you manage Walmart too?

Yes, when you want that channel. Walmart listings, Walmart Connect ads, and WFS can sit with the same team. We do not paste Amazon campaigns onto Walmart. The Walmart marketing page is where that channel is described.
