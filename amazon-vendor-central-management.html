1P vs 3P

Vendor Central vs Seller Central is a different business, not a different skin on the same dashboard. On Vendor Central you are a wholesaler. Amazon sends a purchase order, you ship it, and Amazon sells to the customer at a price Amazon sets. On Seller Central you are the seller. You set the price, you own the customer order, and you replenish FBA or ship it yourself. Amazon 1P management is the first model. Calling it "account management" without saying which login you mean is how scopes get confused.

1P and 3P are different logins. Your vendor agreement can change the margin. We read it on the audit.

PO and chargeback management

A purchase order is not a suggestion you glance at. It has a ship window, a quantity, and a destination. Missing the window or shipping a quantity Amazon did not receive is how a shortage claim starts. We confirm the POs you intend to fill, track the dates, and reconcile what left your warehouse against what Amazon checked in. An unreconciled PO is not "Amazon being Amazon" until somebody opens it.

Vendor Central chargebacks are the invoices Amazon takes back. The types we see most often are shortage claims, price claims, prep or labeling failures, and problems with the advance ship notice or the pickup. Those names are the dispute categories in the account. We confirm which ones are actually hitting you before we build a project around a category you do not have. A dispute needs the document that matches the claim: the PO, the ASN, the carton detail, the invoice. A narrative email does not replace them.

Two jobs sit side by side, and only one of them is a dispute. The first is recovering a claim that should not have been charged. The second is fixing the warehouse step so the next PO does not earn the same claim. We do both. We do not publish a recovery dollar amount or a recovery rate. It depends on the account, the paperwork, and which claims Amazon accepts. Anyone quoting you a percentage before they have logged in is guessing.

What this is not: FBA reimbursement for lost warehouse units. That is a Seller Central problem, covered on FBA management. Vendor shortage claims can look similar on a spreadsheet and they are filed in a different system, against a purchase order.

Catalog control

On a vendor account, Amazon is also a retailer with its own idea of the listing. Item setup, images, titles, and variation families still have to be right, and they still get overwritten. Catalog control means knowing which fields you can edit, which changes Amazon accepted, and which ASINs are live in the vendor catalog versus stranded in a setup error.

Where the vendor account has Brand Registry, A+ Content and a Brand Store can be in scope. The content standard is the same one on our A+ content and listing optimization pages. We confirm what that vendor login can actually publish before we plan modules you cannot submit. Availability is an account fact, not a default.

We also watch for the 1P version of a hijacked page: detail-page edits you did not make, or an offer state that no longer matches the product you are shipping on the PO. Enforcement tools, when the brand is registered, are described on Amazon brand protection. Vendor management uses them. It does not replace them with a purchase order.

Ads for vendors

Ads for vendors are Amazon Ads inside Vendor Central. A lot of brand teams still say AMS. The campaigns, where the account has them, are the same family you would recognize from Seller Central: Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display. Not every vendor account has every type turned on. We look at the login before we propose a structure you cannot build.

The weekly pass is the one described on Amazon PPC management: search terms, negatives, bids, and budgets. The login is the part that changes. We do not paste a Seller Central ACOS story onto a vendor account and call it the same result. The company figure we do cite elsewhere, a 45% average ACOS reduction in the first 90 days, describes the Amazon accounts behind that stat. A vendor account is judged on its own numbers after we can see them.

Retail readiness still gates the ads. A detail page Amazon has suppressed, or a PO you cannot fill, will waste the click. We would rather pause a campaign than buy traffic for an ASIN the vendor catalog cannot sell this week.

Switching or hybrid

Some brands are invited into Vendor Central. Some want out. Some run both and hope the two catalogs do not fight. We do not recommend a switch from this page. On the audit we look at four things: margin after the vendor terms you actually signed, whether Amazon is still sending purchase orders, who holds the Buy Box, and whether Seller Central would give you price control you do not have as a wholesaler.

Co-op, damage allowance, freight, and payment terms live in your agreement. We read them. We do not publish a standard co-op rate, because there is not one we could honestly print. A comparison that ignores those lines will make 3P look better, or worse, than it is.

A hybrid can be the right answer: some ASINs stay on purchase orders, others move to Seller Central where you need to set the price or where Amazon has stopped ordering. It can also be a mess. Two logins, two inventory positions, and no rule for which ASIN lives where, and you end up competing with Amazon on your own product. If we recommend a hybrid, the recommendation includes that rule. The 3P operating week, once an ASIN is on Seller Central, is account management, not a second copy of this page.

Leaving Vendor Central is also operational. Open POs still have to ship or be cancelled cleanly. Chargebacks already issued still have to be disputed. Ads have to be rebuilt in the seller account, not assumed to transfer. We sequence that. We do not flip a switch and call it a migration.

If you are deciding whether to stay 1P, bring the terms. A call without the agreement is a tour of Vendor Central. A call with the agreement is a decision.

FAQ

What is the difference between Vendor Central and Seller Central?

Vendor Central is 1P. You sell wholesale to Amazon on purchase orders, and Amazon is the seller the customer buys from. Seller Central is 3P. Your brand sells to the customer, you set the price, and you fulfill through FBA or merchant fulfillment. The logins, the fees, and the control you keep are different. Which one is better depends on your terms and your catalog, not on a slogan.

What do you do with purchase orders and chargebacks?

We confirm the purchase orders you intend to ship, track the ship window, and reconcile what Amazon received. Chargebacks, including shortage claims, price claims, prep or labeling, and advance ship notice problems, get disputed with the documents that support them. We also fix the warehouse step that caused a repeat claim. We do not publish a recovery dollar amount or a recovery rate.

Can you run ads for a vendor account?

Yes, when the vendor account has Amazon Ads access. Many brand teams still call that AMS. The weekly work is search terms, negatives, bids, and budgets, in the vendor login rather than Seller Central. We do not assume every vendor account has the same ad types turned on. We look first.

Should we stay 1P, move to 3P, or run both?

We will not answer that from a blog post. On the audit we read margin after your vendor terms, whether Amazon is sending purchase orders, who holds the Buy Box, and whether Seller Central would give you price control you do not have now. A hybrid, some ASINs wholesale and some on Seller Central, needs a rule for which ASIN lives where so you are not competing with yourself.

What does Vendor Central management cost?

We do not publish a separate monthly figure on this page. Scope is set after we read the vendor account: purchase order volume, chargeback load, catalog size, and whether ads are included. Ongoing retainers are described on the pricing guide. Amazon's own terms, including co-op or freight if your agreement has them, are paid under that agreement, not to SellTru as a standard rate.
