Amazon Q4 Prep Checklist for 2026 Holiday Sales

By Andrew Deramo August 2026 6 min read

Every January I do a post-mortem on the Q4 accounts we manage, and the pattern never changes: the brands that had a great holiday season won it in September. The brands that had a rough one started thinking about Q4 in late October — after the inventory cutoffs had passed, after the deal windows had closed, and right as CPCs started climbing. Amazon published its 2026 holiday readiness dates on July 7 this year, which means every deadline is already on the calendar. Here is the checklist we run across our managed accounts, in order.

Step 1: Get Inventory In Before the Cutoffs (This Is the Whole Game)

Nothing else on this list matters if your product isn't in an Amazon fulfillment center when shoppers show up. Amazon tightened the inbound windows again this year, and the dates are earlier than most sellers expect.

2026 inbound deadlines: For Prime Big Deal Days in October — AWD shipments by September 2; FBA with minimal shipment splits by September 9; FBA with Amazon-optimized splits by September 16. For Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday — AWD by October 14; FBA by October 21 (minimal splits) or October 28 (optimized splits).

Two things to note. First, "arrive by" means received and checked in, not shipped. Receiving times stretch badly in September and October, so treat these dates as the absolute end of the window, not the target. Second, Amazon's holiday peak fulfillment fees run October 15, 2026 through January 14, 2027 — same fee structure as last year, but it still needs to be in your Q4 margin math before you set ad budgets.

While you're in there, run a stranded and suppressed inventory check: Inventory → Manage Inventory → filter Inactive. A suppressed listing in November is a disaster you could have caught in September. We wrote a full diagnostic in why Amazon sales drop suddenly, and listing suppression is one of the most common causes.

Step 2: Submit Deals Before the Windows Close

Deal submission for Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday runs through October 20. The Prime Big Deal Days window has already closed for this year — it ended September 8 — which is exactly the point: if you're reading this in mid-October, you've already missed one event.

Not every product needs a deep discount. On our accounts, the deal calculus is simple: run Lightning Deals or Best Deals on products where you're fighting for organic rank, and hold price on products that already own their keywords. A deal buys you a burst of velocity and review accumulation; it doesn't have to buy you profit that week. The rank you keep in December is the payoff.

Step 3: Get Listings and Creative Holiday-Ready by Mid-October

Amazon's catalog gets slow and unpredictable in November. Image changes, A+ updates, and variation edits that take hours in September can sit in limbo for days during peak. Lock your creative by mid-October: seasonal main-image angles where appropriate, gift-oriented bullet framing, and A+ content that answers the "is this a good gift" question. Do not make structural listing changes — variation merges, category changes, title rewrites — during Black Friday week. The re-indexing risk isn't worth it.

Step 4: Ramp Ads Into the Climb — Don't Cut When CPCs Rise

Here's the mistake I see every single year. CPCs start climbing in early November, ACoS ticks up, and sellers panic-cut bids right before the highest-converting traffic of the year arrives. Conversion rates during Black Friday and Cyber Monday rise faster than click costs on most of our accounts, which means efficiency often holds or improves even as CPCs spike — but only if you're still in the auction.

This is why we manage Q4 to TACoS, not ACoS. Ad-attributed efficiency looks ugly for a week; total ad spend against total revenue tells the truth. A campaign running hot during Cyber Monday while organic sales double is doing exactly its job.

Structurally, the playbook is the same one from our 2026 PPC strategies breakdown: product targeting on competitor listings (gift shoppers browse comparisons more than any other time of year), Sponsored Brand video on your head terms, and budget caps raised high enough that campaigns don't go dark at 2pm on Black Friday. An out-of-budget campaign during peak is throwing away the exact traffic you spent all year positioning for.

Step 5: Plan the January Landing, Not Just the December Peak

Q4 doesn't end on December 25. Returns spike, peak fees run through January 14, and the brands that spent December at full throttle often wake up in January over-inventoried with a bloated TACoS. Before the season starts, decide what your January bid posture looks like and which products you'll let sell down versus restock. The goal of Q4 isn't a big December — it's a bigger baseline of organic rank you carry into next year.

The bottom line: Q4 is a logistics deadline problem in September, a creative problem in October, and an ad management problem in November — sellers who treat it as one big November problem lose all three.
