# Google's Official Guide: Optimizing for Generative AI in Search

**Source:** [developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)  
**Published:** May 15, 2026  
**Announced via:** [Google Search Central Blog – May 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing)

---

## Overview

User preferences are rapidly evolving and people are increasingly gravitating to generative AI experiences to help them find information. As Google upgrades Search to meet these changing expectations, this transformation offers new opportunities to reach people who may be more inclined to engage with your site, spend more time with your content, or even convert by becoming a subscriber or making a purchase.

This guide is Google's official best practices for website owners on how to succeed in generative AI features in Google Search — specifically **AI Overviews** and **AI Mode**.

---

## Is SEO Still Relevant for Generative AI Search?

**Yes.** The best practices for SEO continue to be relevant because Google's generative AI features are rooted in its core Search ranking and quality systems.

These features rely on two key AI techniques:

### 1. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
Also known as "grounding." A technique used to improve the quality, accuracy, and freshness of AI responses by relying on core Search ranking systems to retrieve relevant, up-to-date web pages from the Search index. Google's systems then review specific information from those retrieved pages to generate a more reliable and helpful response — showing prominent, clickable links to relevant web pages that support the information in the response.

### 2. Query Fan-Out
A set of concurrent, related queries generated by the model to request more information and fetch additional relevant search results to address the user's query.

**Example:** If the original user query is *"how to fix a lawn that's full of weeds"*, fan-out queries might include *"best herbicides for lawns"*, *"remove weeds without chemicals"*, and *"how to prevent weeds in lawn"*.

### What About "AEO" and "GEO"?
- **AEO** = Answer Engine Optimization
- **GEO** = Generative Engine Optimization

These are terms used to describe work specifically focused on improving visibility in AI search experiences. From Google Search's perspective, **optimizing for generative AI search is still just SEO** — it is not a separate discipline.

## What Actually Matters: Foundational SEO Best Practices

### 1. Create Valuable, Non-Commodity Content for Your Audience

This is the single most important factor. Creating content that people find unique, compelling, and useful will likely influence your website's presence in generative AI search more than anything else in this guide.

**What non-commodity content looks like:**

- **Unique point of view:** Google's AI systems look at a variety of sources, so having a unique viewpoint that stands out is helpful. A first-hand review provides a unique perspective based on personal experience, whereas a summary of existing content simply restates information already available elsewhere. Create content based on what you actually know about the topic. Don't just recycle what others on the internet have already said, or what could easily be produced by a generative AI model.

- **Helpful, reliable, people-first content:** Commodity content (e.g., *"7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers"*) is based on common knowledge, which could originate from anyone, and typically adds little unique insight. In contrast, non-commodity content (e.g., *"Why We Waived the Inspection & Saved Money: A Look Inside the Sewer Line"*) provides unique expert or experienced takes that go beyond common knowledge.

- **Well-organized content:** Write content for your human audience and make sure it is well-written and easy to follow. Pages organized by paragraphs and sections, with headings that provide clear structure, are generally appreciated by users.

- **High-quality images and video:** Google's generative AI search features can bring in relevant images and video, meaning more opportunities for your website to appear beyond web page links. When it makes sense, support your textual content with high-quality, relevant images and videos. Following existing image SEO and video SEO best practices already means you're optimizing for generative AI search.

- **Focus on what users want — avoid overdoing it:** Creating separate content for every possible variation of how people might search (e.g., targeting every fan-out query variation) primarily to manipulate rankings violates Google's **scaled content abuse spam policy** and is an ineffective long-term strategy. A high quantity of pages doesn't make a website higher quality. Google's AI systems have advanced in their ability to understand the relevance of pages even when there is no exact match between the query and the page's primary content.

- **Using AI tools to help create content:** If you use generative AI tools in content creation, be sure the work meets the standards of Search Essentials and Google's spam policies.

**The simplest test:** *"Is this content that my visitors would find satisfying?"* If yes, you're on the right track.

---

### 2. Build and Maintain a Clear Technical Structure

The way Google Search finds and processes your pages remains the core of how its AI systems access your data. Technical clarity ensures your content is ready for discovery and indexing.

Key technical requirements:

- **Meet the Search technical requirements:** To be eligible to appear in generative AI features, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet. Just because a page meets all requirements and policies doesn't guarantee it will be crawled, indexed, or served — indexing and serving are not guaranteed.

- **Follow crawling best practices:** Ensure your content is crawlable. Google's generative AI models use publicly accessible, crawlable content to learn patterns and provide relevant, grounded responses. For very large and frequently updated sites, review Google's guide to optimizing crawl budget.

- **Semantic HTML:** It's not required to have perfectly semantic HTML (the web in general is not valid HTML, and Google can understand it). However, it's generally a good idea to use semantic HTML when possible, as it helps other types of users (such as screen readers) parse and navigate your web page more easily — and this matters for agentic experiences.

- **JavaScript SEO:** Google can process content within JavaScript as long as it isn't blocked. Working on SEO with a JavaScript framework is generally more complex than with other kinds of websites. Follow standard JavaScript SEO best practices.

- **Good page experience:** Ensure your site displays well across all devices, reduce latency, and make it easy for people to distinguish your main content from other elements on the page.

- **Reduce duplicate content:** Duplicate content can be a bad user experience, and search engines might waste crawling resources on URLs you don't care about.

Use **Google Search Console** to discover and diagnose potential technical issues.

### 3. Optimize Your Local Business and Ecommerce Details

Where appropriate, generative AI responses can include product listings, product information, and information about local businesses.

- **Merchant Center** (including Merchant Center feeds) and **Google Business Profiles** can help your products and services be visible in both AI responses and other Google Search results.
- Depending on your business type and goals, consider other merchant experiences like **Business Agent** — a conversational experience on Google Search that helps customers chat with your brand.

## Mythbusting: What You Do NOT Need to Do

Google explicitly calls out several commonly suggested "AI SEO hacks" that are ineffective or unsupported by how Google Search actually works:

### ❌ LLMS.txt files and other "special" markup
You do **not** need to create new machine-readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in generative AI search. Note: Google may discover, crawl, and index many kinds of files in addition to HTML — but this doesn't mean the file is treated in a special way.

### ❌ "Chunking" content
There's **no requirement** to break your content into tiny pieces for AI to better understand it. Google's systems can understand the nuance of multiple topics on a page and show the relevant piece to users. There's no ideal page length — make pages for your audience, not just for generative AI search.

### ❌ Rewriting content just for AI systems
You do **not** need to write in a specific way just for generative AI search. AI systems can understand synonyms and general meanings of what someone is seeking, in order to connect them with content that might not use the same precise words. You don't need to worry about capturing every variation of how someone might search.

### ❌ Seeking inauthentic "mentions"
Google's core ranking systems focus on high-quality content while other systems block spam. Seeking inauthentic "mentions" across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem — and Google's systems are specifically designed to detect and discount this.

### ❌ Overfocusing on structured data
Structured data is **not required** for generative AI search, and there's **no special schema.org markup** you need to add. However, it's still a good idea to use structured data as part of your overall SEO strategy, as it helps with eligibility for rich results in Google Search.

## Exploring Agentic Experiences (Emerging — Optional)

AI agents are autonomous systems that can perform tasks on behalf of people, such as booking a reservation or comparing product specifications. Browser agents may access your website to gather data by:
- Analyzing visual renderings (like screenshots)
- Inspecting the DOM structure
- Interpreting the accessibility tree

For businesses interested in this space, Google recommends reviewing the guide to **agent-friendly website best practices** (available at web.dev) and watching the emerging **Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP)** standard.

---

## Key Takeaways (Google's Summary)

1. **Apply SEO best practices to generative AI search:** Continue prioritizing foundational SEO — building a clear technical structure and creating unique, valuable content. These are the foundation for visibility in generative AI search experiences.

2. **Create non-commodity, people-first content:** Focus on developing unique, expert-led content that provides value beyond common knowledge.

3. **Prioritize effective SEO over AEO/GEO hacks:** For Google Search, ignore tactics like chunking content, creating unnecessary AI text files (like llms.txt), or pursuing inauthentic mentions.

4. **Explore agentic experiences:** Stay informed about emerging technologies that allow AI agents to interact with your site, such as browser agents and new protocols.
